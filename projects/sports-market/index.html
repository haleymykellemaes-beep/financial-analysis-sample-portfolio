Sports market · expected value

The price is a probability plus a margin.

American odds convert to a break-even probability. Two break-evens that sum to more than one are the book’s posted margin. Realized hold is a different number: it depends on which side the money was on. Expected value below uses a model probability you can move. It is not a claim that the model is right.

Research exhibit. No bet is recommended. Positive model EV means the probability you supplied clears the posted price. It is not a recorded edge, and nothing on this page is a season-long result.

01 · Price

Two American prices, and the margin between them

Negative odds −O imply O / (O + 100). Positive odds +O imply 100 / (O + 100). The overround is the sum minus one. Proportional de-vig divides each side by the sum. Additive de-vig subtracts half the overround from each side. They agree on a coin flip and diverge when one side is a longshot.

02 · Distribution

A model margin, drawn as a normal

The case study turns a point margin into a win probability at 3 percentage points per point. That rule is the tangent, at a pick’em, of a normal margin distribution. The sigma on the slider starts at the value that makes the tangent true. It is not estimated from a file of scores. Moving it is a sensitivity, not a fit.

04 · Scores on documented inputs

Four readings, two games

Brier score is the squared gap between the probability and the 0/1 result. Log loss is the negative log of the probability on the result. Lower is better on both. Three of the four rows are Super Bowl LIX read three ways, so a lower score there is one game, not three pieces of evidence.

Every subject in the scored set won. A reliability curve needs losses. The chart draws the algebraic identity Brier = (1 − p)² for an event that happened. The dots sit on that identity. They were not fit.

06 · Limits

What this can and cannot support

  • The independent model is a mean margin, not a simulation of drives, injuries, or weather. Sigma is implied by the 3% rule. Changing sigma does not create a calibrated football model.
  • SRS inputs are the figures cited in the portfolio case study. They were not re-downloaded from Pro-Football-Reference for this page.
  • The LIX moneyline is the pair stated in that case study. A single closing print from every book was not archived here. The Broncos pair is a BetMGM snapshot at 12:27 p.m. ET, about three and a half hours before kickoff.
  • De-vig is an assumption about how the book split its margin. The gap between the proportional and additive numbers is uncertainty about that split.
  • Nevada’s 14.6% is the share of Super Bowl handle the books won. It is not the overround on the moneyline, and it is not evidence of a repeatable betting advantage.
  • No path on this page was generated by simulating bettors, seasons, or bankrolls. There is no performance record to report.
