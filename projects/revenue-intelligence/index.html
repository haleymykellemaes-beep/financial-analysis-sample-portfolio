What was measured

Merchandise net sales are gross merchandise lines, plus returns, plus discount and commission lines. Across the whole extract that is £18,905,453. The 24 complete months are £18,466,186. Returns were 1.8% of merchandise gross. Postage, £433,411 signed, is kept out of the target. The source is Chen, D. (2012), Online Retail II, UCI Machine Learning Repository.

The forecast that survived

On 12 one-step forecasts from December 2010 through November 2011, repeating the same month last year missed by £65,495 on average, or 8.4% of revenue. Last month missed by £193,678. A drift model missed by £197,875. A trend-plus-cycle regression missed by £225,932. The base plan does not add a growth trend the data did not reward.

December, and the range around it

Both complete Decembers stop on 23 December. Their average, £768,000, sits with an ordinary month. November is the peak: its average was £1.41 million, 1.83 times the average complete month. For a full December after November 2011 the selected model says £757,330. An approximate 80% residual band is £649,796 to £864,865. A separate growth scenario, from the 10th and 90th percentiles of observed year-over-year changes, is £687,609 to £901,564. The band is not widened for months that were not walk-forward tested.

Where the book sits

Identified customers are 86% of net sales. Among positive identified sales, the top decile is 63%, 273 customers account for half, and repeat customers are 97%. The median identified customer is £844. The mean is £2,802. The top 10 products are 7.6% of positive product net sales, so the concentration is in customers, not in a handful of items.
